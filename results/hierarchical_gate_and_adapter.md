# Two open mechanisms: a conditional detector, and a lost observation

## 1. The AutoGen adapter was discarding agent output — fixed

`run_autogen_episode` handled three AutoGen events and ignored a fourth.
`ThoughtEvent` carries, in the framework's own words, "the extra text content
generated by a function call" -- the model's commentary that accompanies a
tool call rather than replacing it.

Measured on the committed corpora before the fix:

| framework | tool_call steps | carrying assistant text | median prefix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LangGraph | 838 | 202 (24%) | 135 chars |
| AutoGen | 764 | **0** | -- |

This is not a difference in agent behaviour. Probing Ollama directly on the
turn after an unhelpful tool result returns both together:

```
content   : 'None of the papers in the search results match the query about
             echo state networks for anomaly detection. Let me try another
             search.'
tool_calls: [arxiv_search({"query": "... machine learning"})]
```

LangChain surfaces that content as `msg.content` and the LangGraph runner
records it. AutoGen surfaces it as `CreateResult.thought` -> `ThoughtEvent`,
and the runner dropped the event on the floor.

**What makes this matter rather than merely being untidy:** the text appears
precisely when the model comments on a tool result that did not help -- which
is the wrong_document, context_corruption and goal_drift situation. The
observation most relevant to detecting those failures was the one being
discarded, and only under one framework.

**Fixed** by buffering the thought and prefixing it onto the step that carries
the calls, which is exactly where LangGraph puts the same content. It does not
become a step of its own: it is the same agent turn, and emitting an extra
step would manufacture scoring opportunities the agent never took. Verified
live -- on a six-step episode against a tool returning irrelevant content, two
tool_call steps now carry 100 and 125 characters of the agent's own reasoning
that would previously have been lost. Three regression tests pin the capture,
the no-extra-step rule, and the buffer reset.

**Not yet measured: whether this improves detection.** The committed
`autogen7b_real2` corpus was collected under the old adapter, so the benefit
cannot be measured without re-collecting that development corpus. That is a
live collection and it replaces spent development evidence, so it is a
decision rather than something to slip in.

## 2. The conditional design: the budget holds once it is calibrated correctly

The flat design cannot afford the goal-consistency channel: adding it costs
about thirty points on every other class. The conditional argument is that
rates compose -- a gate opening on a quarter of healthy episodes, followed by
a test firing on a fifth of those, spends 5% in total while letting the second
test operate at 20%.

`P(alarm) = P(stage 1) x P(stage 2 | stage 1)` is the chain rule. It is exact
and requires no independence between the stages. An earlier version of this
document attributed an overshoot to the stages being correlated; that was
wrong, and the real cause was an error in how the thresholds were estimated.

**The estimation defect.** Both thresholds were read off the whole healthy
pool, which contained the episodes the readout had been fitted on. A fitted
episode carries a residual the fit already minimised, so a threshold taken
from one sits too low, and the unseen population overshoots it. The signature
was visible in stage 1 alone, before any question of conditioning arose: a
gate calibrated for 25% fired on 59% of held-out healthy episodes.

Corrected to three disjoint healthy folds -- fit, scale, and a calibration
fold the readout never saw -- the composition holds:

| corpus | allocation | gate FA (target) | total FA | detection | drift realized | conditional fold n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| langgraph | 0.25 x 0.20 | 0.234 (0.25) | 0.031 | 0.188 | 0.517 | 6.0 |
| langgraph | 0.50 x 0.10 | 0.657 (0.50) | 0.044 | 0.223 | 0.683 | 17.2 |
| autogen | 0.25 x 0.20 | 0.243 (0.25) | 0.033 | 0.259 | 0.600 | 5.2 |
| autogen | 0.50 x 0.10 | 0.641 (0.50) | 0.059 | 0.287 | **0.917** | 13.5 |
| long_drift | 0.05 x 1.00 | 0.024 (0.05) | 0.011 | 0.459 | 0.573 | 1.0 |
| long_drift | 0.25 x 0.20 | 0.235 (0.25) | 0.021 | 0.194 | 0.296 | 6.1 |

Total false alarms now land between 0.003 and 0.059 against a 5% budget, and
the gate tracks its nominal rate at 0.25. The conditional budget works.

**What does not work is this particular hierarchy, for a different reason.**
It is built from `g` and `c` alone and therefore *replaces* the flat detector
rather than complementing it. The classes that depend on `e` and `m` collapse:
looping falls to 0.208 on langgraph against 0.967 under `e+m+g/mean`, and
overall detection to 0.223 against 0.581. Realized goal drift rises sharply in
exchange -- 0.683 against 0.117 -- which is the trade the conditional design
was meant to avoid having to make.

The design that follows from this has not been measured: keep the flat
`e+m`-based detector for the classes it already serves and add the `g` gate to
`c` path beside it, splitting the 5% budget between the two rather than
choosing between them. That is the next experiment, and it is named here rather
than run.

### The remaining blocker, restated correctly

The budget arithmetic holds, and the conditional fold is not the constraint it
was taken to be. A threshold at the `k`-th largest of `n` healthy values fires
on fresh healthy episodes at a rate distributed `Beta(k, n + 1 - k)`, so the
precision of any false-alarm calibration is governed by the number of
calibration episodes above the final threshold. For the flat detector that is
`0.05 x N`; for the two-stage detector it is `p2 x (p1 x N)`, which is
`0.05 x N` as well. The two designs need the same calibration fold, and a
conditional fold of 20 resolving a 20% rate is exactly as precise as a fold of
80 resolving 5%.

So the earlier figure of roughly 320 healthy episodes per deployment was wrong:
it required the conditional fold to match the flat protocol's 80-episode floor,
when what has to match is the exceedance count, not the fold size. At the same
precision the frozen flat detector already runs at -- 4 exceedances -- the
conditional design needs 80 calibration episodes and yields a conditional fold
of 20. The deployment corpus already holds that.

What neither design holds is precision better than that. Four exceedances put
the 95th percentile of the realized false alarm rate at 9.4% against a 5%
nominal; reaching a 1.5x bound takes 13 exceedances, or 260 calibration
episodes. The single-digit conditional folds in the table above are worse
still: at one exceedance the threshold is the largest value in the fold, which
is not a calibration at all.

The gating question for the complementary design is therefore not how to size
its second stage but what calibration precision the deployment is required to
demonstrate. Sized against the flat detector's existing standard it is already
runnable; sized against a 1.5x bound it needs 477 healthy episodes per
deployment and so does the flat detector. That choice is not the detector's to
make, so the design stays unrun and the 5% budget stays as it is. The sizing
derivation is in `conditional_calibration_sizing.md`.

Recording this as the state: goal drift stays OPEN, the conditional design
stays a live candidate with its real requirement quantified, and `c` stays
rejected for global use.
